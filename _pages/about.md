Nathaniel Neal completed his undergraduate degree in Zoology and Geographic Information Systems at Oregon State University. While at OSU, he worked as a field technician in the Oregon Coast Range, leading to the construction and successful defense of an Honor's Thesis on ecological associations of the Pacific Giant Salamander (_Dicapmtodon tenebrosus_).

Following graduation in 2022, Nathaniel began a Master's degree in Environmental Science at Washington State University, Vancouver. This is where Nathaniel was first introduced to freshwater mussels and they became the focus of his research. The emphasis of his Master's thesis was on the ecological associations of native freshwater mussels (_Gonidea angulata_ and _Margaritifera falcata_) and the invasive Asian clam (_Corbicula fluminea_).

Nathaniel is now a PhD student co-advised by Dr. Jingchun Li and Dr. Scott A. Taylor at the University of Colorado, Boulder, following his graduation from WSU in 2025. Shifting from ecological associations to genomics and the genetic basis of complex traits, Nathaniel now studies complex lures in freshwater mussels.
